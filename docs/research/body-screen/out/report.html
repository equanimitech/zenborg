Body and Screen

Does the body's stress signal (Garmin) track how restlessly the hands move across apps and tabs (macOS daemon)? Six hypotheses, 75 days of physiology, 78 days of desktop trace, 333 hours where both were observed at the computer.

Bonferroni over six hypotheses puts the bar at α = 0.0083. Hourly rows are autocorrelated within a day; regression p-values use Newey–West (HAC, 3 lags). Nothing here generalises beyond this one gardener.

H2Overnight HRV does not predict the next day's dynamism

Cleanest design first: last night's HRV (from readiness_recorded) against the total app + tab switches of the day that followed. Spearman ρ = -0.11 (p = 0.45); per active hour ρ = -0.099. Controlling for weekend and sleep score, the standardised HRV coefficient is β = -0.09 (p = 0.62, R² = 0.082). Normalising HRV against Garmin's balanced range (n = 39) gives ρ = -0.18, p = 0.27. The placebo — the night after — is indistinguishable (ρ = -0.063). HRV also fails to predict the day's mean stress (ρ = -0.093).

Overnight HRV (ms) → switches that day. One point per day; line is OLS.

H1Within the hour, stress and switching are unrelated — but app and tab switching pull opposite ways

Hourly mean stress against switches per active minute: r = 0.069 raw, r = 0.03 after residualising both on hour-of-day and weekday (p = 0.59). HAC regression: 0.245 stress points per extra switch/min, p = 0.64. The composite hides a split: app switches correlate weakly positively with stress (ρ = 0.125, p = 0.02) while tab switches correlate negatively (ρ = -0.177, p = 0.001). Browsing hours are calmer than terminal-and-editor hours. Mouse-move rate carries nothing (ρ = -0.03).

H3Input entropy does not rise with stress

Shannon entropy over the four input channels (keys, clicks, moves, scrolls) per 30 s bin, averaged into Garmin's 5-minute stress bins. ρ = 0.043 (p = 0.03, n = 2565); partial rank correlation controlling for total input volume ρ = 0.059. Statistically visible at this n, practically nothing: entropy explains under 0.2% of stress variance. Entropy is mostly a function of how much input there is (ρ = 0.336 with volume).

Input entropy (bits) → stress, 5-minute bins outside workouts.

H4Body-battery drain looks tied to dynamism, until hour-of-day is removed

Raw: r = -0.194 (p = < 0.001) — busier hours drain more. Residualised on hour and weekday: r = -0.017 (p = 0.75). The whole effect is circadian: mornings recharge, afternoons drain, and switching happens to peak in the morning. Adding stress as a control leaves r = 0.004. Note body battery is computed from stress (r = -0.616 here), so it can never be an independent witness.

Switches per active minute → body battery change over the hour (negative = drain).

Method and caveats

Alignment. Garmin body_sampled records were re-keyed on their epoch timestamp (the hour field disagrees between sync batches) and shifted back one hour after verifying against the raw Garmin series on two dates; the vault stamps the end of the sampled hour. The Mac and watch moved from Europe/Paris to UTC−3 on Aug 17; hour-of-day and day boundaries follow that switch.

Backfill. Garmin returns no data before Jun 25 (first day worn), so the window is bounded by the device, not the logs. The vault's own sync had already filled Jun 25 → Sep 8.

Filters. Hourly rows need ≥ 10 active minutes (60 minus idle spans), ≥ 6 of 12 stress bins, and no overlapping workout or Garmin ACTIVITY event. Daily rows need ≥ 60 active minutes and a recorded overnight HRV.

What would change the answer. Task labels — the tab-vs-app split suggests what is being switched between matters more than how often. A longer window for H2 (50 days at ρ ≈ −0.1 has ~10% power for a small effect). And an independent arousal channel: body battery is derived from stress, so H4 is not a second test.
